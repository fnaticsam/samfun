A path in ten stages, and the reference behind it

You were never bound. Finding that out is the work.

Awakening is not a distant achievement. It is the moment the one watching the drama stops believing it is the character on the screen. This site lays out how that mistaken identity is built, how it is undone, and how the ancient teachings of Buddhism, the Vedas, the Tao, the Sufis and the Christian mystics all point at the same thing.

Walk the path

Ten stages, in order. Each one explains a piece of the mechanism in plain words, ends with a practice you can do today, and a short self-check.

Go deeper

Every stage carries expandable "Go deeper" sections: the Sanskrit and Pali terms, the parallel teachings in each tradition, and the finer distinctions the short version leaves out.

Use the reference

A field guide to what actually comes up in practice, a glossary, a map across five traditions, and an index of the analogies used throughout.

The ten stages

Stage 1The CurrentWhat awakening is and is not, why it is worth it, and the one case of mistaken identity behind all suffering. Stage 2How a Self Is BuiltThe five aggregates, watched in slow motion with a mosquito. Plato's cave, and why energy follows attention. Stage 3MomentumSamskaras, karma as inertia, the ceiling fan that keeps spinning, and the two knots of comfort and knowing. Stage 4SittingWhat every meditation technique has in common. Concentration, attention, awareness. Hindrances as friends. Stage 5No PreferencesThe Great Way, the Tao that yields like water, the farmer who says "maybe", and the disease of the mind. Stage 6AbsorptionThe jhanas, samadhi with a seed, peak experiences that create a seeker, and why the mind can never remember it. Stage 7Who Am I?Direct inquiry. The dyad, the imperative that is not a question, and the game you only win by giving up. Stage 8Fear and the ShadowPrimordial fear as contracted aliveness. The hero's cave, the serpents around the Buddha, and the two horses. Stage 9Beyond ConceptsSelf and no-self as equal traps, the Buddha's silence, the spiritual persona, and the still point that cannot be reached by moving. Stage 10Living ItIntegration, countless small deaths, the fourth state, the new creation, and the mirror mind that meets each moment fresh.

How to use this

Read the stages in order the first time; each one assumes the last. The short teaching at the top of every stage is enough to work with. The "Go deeper" sections are the same territory at full resolution, for when you want the scholarship, the original terms, and the cross-tradition parallels. Nothing in the deep layer is required to do the practice.

Treat every concept here as a tool to be used and then put down. The teachings are a menu, not the meal. As one Zen master warned, mistaking the map for the territory is the most common way of getting lost on this path, and it can happen with this very page.

One caution before starting: this work loosens the grip of the conditioned self, and that can feel intense. The field guide covers what to expect. If you are in a period of psychological instability, work with a qualified teacher or therapist alongside anything you find here.
