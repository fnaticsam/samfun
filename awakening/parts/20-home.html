A recurring invitation

A perennialist view of contemplative traditions.

Islam · Sufism

Fana · Baqa

The sense of a separate "I" falls away in absorption in God; life becomes an abiding in divine presence.

Hinduism · Advaita Vedanta

Atma-jnana · Moksha

Awakening beyond identification with body and mind to recognize the true Self as Brahman, ultimate reality.

Hinduism · Kashmir Shaivism

Pratyabhijna

Recognizing one's true nature as Shiva, the universal consciousness appearing as all experience.

Buddhism

Bodhi · Nirvana

Awakening from ignorance and clinging to "I" and "mine," bringing liberation from suffering.

Buddhism · Zen / Chan

Kensho · Satori

Directly seeing one's true nature, beyond conceptual thinking and attachment to a separate self.

Dzogchen · Tibetan Buddhism & Bön

Rigpa

Recognizing the empty, luminous nature of awareness beyond identification with passing thoughts.

Taoism

Ming · Returning to the root

Returning to the source through clarity and stillness, living naturally in accord with the Dao.

Christian mysticism

Theosis · Divine union

Surrendering self-will and entering transformative union with God through love and grace.

Jewish mysticism

Bittul · Devekut

The surrender of independent self-importance and a deep attachment to divine presence.

Sikhism

Sahaj · Jivan mukti

Freedom from ego-bound living, abiding in natural spiritual awareness and attunement to the Divine.

Jainism

Kevala-jnana · Moksha

The soul's true knowing becomes unobstructed as karmic bondage is removed, culminating in liberation.

Neoplatonism · Plotinus

Henosis

Transcending ordinary self-awareness in union with the One, the source of all existence.

Shared patterns of realization, expressed through different understandings of self, reality, and the divine.

How to use this

Read the stages in order the first time; each one assumes the last. The short teaching at the top of every stage is enough to work with. The "Go deeper" sections are the same territory at full resolution, for when you want the scholarship, the original terms, and the cross-tradition parallels. Nothing in the deep layer is required to do the practice.

Treat every concept here as a tool to be used and then put down. The teachings are a menu, not the meal. As one Zen master warned, mistaking the map for the territory is the most common way of getting lost on this path, and it can happen with this very page.

One caution before starting: this work loosens the grip of the conditioned self, and that can feel intense. The field guide covers what to expect. If you are in a period of psychological instability, work with a qualified teacher or therapist alongside anything you find here.
