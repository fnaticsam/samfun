A note on the map

Two cautions. First, these are correspondences, not equations. The yoga sutras and the Buddhist jhana maps agree about ninety percent of the time, which is astonishing for independent systems, but the ten percent is real and the traditions argue about it. Second, the map is itself a concept, and Stage 9 applies to it. A person can memorise every column and be no closer than when they started. The table's only use is that when something arises in your own practice, you can recognise it in five languages and be less afraid.

The deepest agreement is the simplest. Every one of these traditions holds that what is being sought is already present, that the seeker is what obscures it, and that the way is a subtraction rather than an acquisition. The Tao Te Ching says it in a line: in the pursuit of the Tao, every day something is dropped.
