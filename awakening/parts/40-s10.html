Living It

Awakening is the beginning of the path, not its end. What follows is integration: the long, unglamorous, sometimes uncomfortable work of letting the whole human vessel catch up with what has been seen, until every moment is met fresh.

In plain words

The flip can happen in a moment. Stabilising it takes countless small deaths, daily vigilance, and a willingness to feel everything life brings up, which will be more than before. The goal is a mirror mind: reflecting each moment without the distortion of memory. The old has passed; everything is new.

Countless small deaths

The pattern after any real glimpse is predictable enough to be a law. The mind comes back online. The ego drafts a story around the experience, takes ownership of it, and weaves it into a narrative: my awakening. Then the state fades and the story remains, a memory added to the self-structure, and the person starts talking about awakening as if they know. To stabilise and integrate samadhi requires, in the words of the tradition, countless awakenings, countless humblings of the mind, countless little deaths. Each time the mind claims the insight, it has to be seen and released again. This is not failure. It is the fan slowing down.

What sustains it is steady practice, sadhana, day in and day out: sitting, inquiry, watching the mind, freeing the inner energy so that the rewiring can proceed and the inner lotus can grow. Not practice as a technique to be performed, but as continuous presence, the recognition of being, carried into whatever is happening. If you are walking, walk. If you are eating, eat. In the seeing, only seeing. It does not require a cushion, though the cushion helps.

Feeling life

Here is what nobody says at the start: the more work you do on the cushion, the more feelings will come up in life. Not fewer. As the reducing valve opens, what it was filtering out arrives, and the practice must be applied there, in the kitchen and the meeting and the argument, or it is only half a practice.

The instruction is simple to state. Life triggers a stored knot, a samskara, and the body tightens: chest, solar plexus, throat. The habit is to close, to defend, to rationalise, to suppress, to reach for distraction. Instead: relax the body, soften the heart, and let the energy pass through. Do not close. Have a complete experience of the sensation, feeling and emotion as it moves, equanimous and inwardly surrendered. Every thorn you allow to be felt fully is one you no longer have to build your life around avoiding.

This takes brutal honesty, because the ego is extraordinarily skilled at hiding. Watch how it defends its worldview, presents itself in a good light, needs to be right, avoids the unpleasant, clings to the familiar, and feeds itself entertainment to avoid the present. It is entirely possible to be stuck in patterns and archetypes, playing out karma, while quoting non-dual teachings. The tell is always the same: is something being felt, or avoided?

Back in the world

Integration meets its real test in relationships and work, and a few things are worth knowing in advance.

Family and friends know the character. When you stop behaving as the character they know, there is friction. This does not have to be a split; it can be this and that. Trust the inner direction, make the pattern conscious, and stop trying to manage everyone's dynamics; the mind cannot do it and will shut down trying. "No thoughts, no problems. I don't mind what happens."

Other people's pain is contagious. When someone else's pain, what Eckhart Tolle calls the pain body, lands on you and your own picks it up, the mind loves to replay the story. Instead, feel how it moves in the body first, keep attention there, and do not feed the thoughts. The wave is big at first and then smaller each time. Practitioners who have learned not to believe their own thoughts also become good at not believing other people's.

Work and money. The anxious loop of "what should I do for work now?" is a thought pattern like any other. Two questions cut through it: what do you actually want to experience in this game, and what are you willing to pay for it? Where attention goes, energy flows, and a clear answer ("I want to help stressed people slow down; I would do it all day") is usually already known and being blocked by "but how would I make money?". The transitions can take years, and the mind will try to repurpose old patterns in new costumes; catch it when it does.

The energy makes corrections. After a real awakening there is, in practice, no going back into the old shape. Practitioners who have tried report that the freed energy will not allow it: illness, collapse of relationships that only worked for the character, an inner correction of some kind. This is not punishment; it is the current asserting itself. It is also different for everyone.

New abilities. Intuition, creativity, sometimes healing or perception that was not there before: the yogic texts call these siddhis, and warn about them. Do not push them away; do not build an identity on them. A tree needs branches to be healthy, and it still needs to grow toward the sun. When the branches become the whole tree, the path is forgotten.

And expect a period, often uncomfortable, when everything the character says sounds false to you. That is fine. It is part of letting go of what needs to be let go of.

The mirror mind

A mirror reflects whatever is in front of it without holding onto it and without preferring one image to another. The mind that meets each moment this way is called, in Zen, beginner's mind; in the inquiry traditions, the don't-know mind; here, the mirror mind. It is the very essence of meditation and self-inquiry, and it is the destination of this path: not a state, but a way of meeting reality without the mediation of past conditioning.

When you are not experiencing through the filter of memory, each moment is new. Every religion has a word for this. The Christian one is exact: "If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. Old things have passed away; behold, all things have become new." And: unless you become as little children, you will not enter the kingdom. Not childish, but the child's spontaneity, the ease of being that has not yet been strategised. Rumi said silence is the language of God and everything else is poor translation, and yet there is a way for these human vessels to speak from silence, freely, without a little mediator managing the effect. That is authenticity: not a better performance, but life coming through unedited.

Compassion arrives here as a byproduct, not an achievement. Having found out who you are, you have found out who everyone is. A moral code is what a society builds when that direct knowing has been lost; it is downstream of the thing itself. Repair the split at its root and compassion is simply how energy moves through an undivided vessel.

Freedom from, freedom to

Everything up to now has been freedom from: from the character, from suffering, from the fan. There is also freedom to. The awakened person is still here, in the dream, and the dream has other possibilities in it: to evolve, to grow this particular vessel in its unique way, to explore what the traditions call the higher worlds and the other dimensions of ourselves. The energy in the spine, the kundalini, is our evolutionary potential; the centres it moves through, the chakras, are the developing parts of the instrument. At the start the human journey is about survival. Then it expands: relationships, family, creating things in the world, an ever-larger sphere of compassion, until the sense of "I" includes everyone and everything. It is all you.

The highest samadhi, the traditions say, is when this awakened consciousness and the time-bound part of us become one: the vessel matured until awareness shines through it without interruption, a transparent expression of the whole. Always being Buddha; always becoming Buddha. The show goes on. The witness is at peace. The mask is off, and the play is better for it.

Growing the lotus

Kundalini is the evolutionary energy already moving through a human life; meditation accelerates it. Whenever a process of mind drops away, the energy tied up in running it is freed. Dhyana, jhana, Chan and Zen are one travelling word, rooted in burning: meditation burns conditioning, and what was bound becomes available.

Every awakening carries some activation of this force, but the two unfold on different clocks. Awakening is formless and only now; it needs no stage of development. Energy belongs to form and develops through time. The old trinity helps hold both. Purusha is pure awareness, the Father. Prakriti is animating spirit or Mother, the energetic principle Christianity often leaves unnamed. Ishvara is the Son: big mind awake in form. Together they are the jewel in the lotus — awareness shining through a flowering human instrument.

The crown is pictured as a thousand-petalled lotus because the unfolding has no tidy end. Maslow’s ladder finishes at self-actualisation; Sri Aurobindo’s account of human evolution begins there. Attachment to any rung slows the movement, whether the attachment is to safety, a beautiful state or a spiritual belief added to the self-structure. You can awaken at any stage, and awakening itself accelerates growth. Always being Buddha; always becoming Buddha.

Ancient images place nāgas, serpents from the waters of the unconscious, calmly at attention around an awakened being. Nothing has been killed. What was hidden now serves awareness. Guanyin rides the dragon rather than slaying it: integrated power at her back, not an enemy under her feet.

The opening is personal. An acorn becomes an oak, but the oak grows differently on a hillside and in a sheltered valley. In the same way, one lotus flowers through art, another through family, service, solitude or an ordinary job. Environment shapes the tree without supplying its inner instruction.

The seven serpents rise calmly around the seated figure, attentive to the lotus opening above.

Some people feel heat, movement, visions or strong currents; others follow the “dry path”, awakening by direct insight without much energetic display. Neither is more real. Chakras do not open as a reliable linear checklist, and different maps count and describe them differently. Turn any map into a programme and the ego has another game to win. Let the actual life teach you. The more awake you are, the more freely the lotus unfolds; the more it unfolds, the more transparent the vessel becomes. There is no need to rush the flower. Enjoy the journey.

Go deeperThe trinity in three languages

The three prongs of Shiva’s trident hold one pattern. In Vedic language they are Purusha, Prakriti and Ishvara: pure awareness, the power that animates form, and awareness embodied as big mind. In Christian language they are Father, Spirit and Son, though the Spirit’s maternal, energetic character is often obscured.

In plain language: awareness, energy, awakened form. Awareness without form is the jewel; energy is the lotus’s living growth; awakened form is the jewel shining through the open flower. These are not three substances stacked together. They are three ways of meeting one reality: what never changes, the movement by which worlds appear, and a life transparent to both. The trident is useful when it keeps formless awakening distinct from development through time, and should be put down when it becomes another diagram to defend.

To find the truth that you know not, you must go by a path that you know not.

St John of the Cross
Go deeperTuriya: awareness through sleep

The Mandukya Upanishad describes three ordinary states, waking, dreaming and deep sleep, and a fourth, turiya, which is not a state alongside them but the awareness underlying all three. In periods of intensive practice, practitioners sometimes discover that awareness remains when the mind drops off at night: the conditioned self is gone, and something is still present. This cannot be made to happen; it is a byproduct of continuous inquiry. Ramana Maharshi described it as a place where there is no more coming and going, an opening into the timeless, for as long as it lasts. Its stabilised, open-eyed form is sahaja, the natural state, in which primordial awareness is recognised as ever-present while life and death arise within it.

Turiya is the experiential answer to the question of Stage 9: what does not change? Not the waking self, which dissolves each night. Not the dreamer. Not the blank of deep sleep. What knows all three.

BuddhismHinduismSufismChristianityTaoism

Zen: the ten ox-herding pictures end not with the empty circle of realisation but with the tenth image, "returning to the marketplace with open hands": an ordinary person in the world, and everyone they meet becomes enlightened. Integration is the last picture, not the eighth.

Hinduism: the jivanmukta is one liberated while living, moving in the world without bondage. The Gita's ideal is the person of steady wisdom who acts fully and claims nothing.

Sufism: after fana comes baqa, subsistence: the self has been annihilated and the servant returns to serve, "in the world but not of it". The Sufi stays in the caravanserai; the saint sells cloth in the bazaar.

Christianity: the new creation of 2 Corinthians 5:17; Paul's "it is no longer I who live"; the kingdom within, lived out. The mystics who finished the dark night did not leave the world; Teresa reformed convents and John wrote in a prison cell.

Taoism: the sage is indistinguishable from an ordinary person, "muddled and murky like everyone else", and does nothing, and nothing is left undone. Before enlightenment, chop wood, carry water; after enlightenment, chop wood, carry water.

Practice: relax, release, and the evening mirror

Part one: in life, whenever something closes you. Part two: five minutes before sleep, every night.

  1. Notice the closing. Someone says the thing, the email arrives, the memory surfaces. Feel the tightening before you feel the story: chest, belly, throat, jaw.
  2. Relax the body. Drop the shoulders, soften the belly, loosen the jaw. Do not wait until you feel better; do it while it hurts.
  3. Keep the heart open. Do not defend, rationalise or suppress. Let the energy of the moment move through the chest instead of being held there. Feel it completely. It passes faster when felt than when managed.
  4. Speak or act from the other side of it, if speaking or acting is needed. Notice how different that is from speaking from inside the contraction.
  5. Evening mirror. Before sleep, replay the day once, briefly. Where did the character defend itself, need to be right, avoid something, present a good face, reach for distraction? Name each honestly, without punishment. Then let the whole day go. Rest as what watched it. Tomorrow is new.

If the same closing keeps returning, it is a groove, and it will keep returning until it has been fully felt. That is not a failure of the practice. It is the practice.

Self-check

Why does life get more intense, not calmer, after real practice?
Because the reducing valve is opening. What it filtered out now arrives to be felt. The practice has to be applied in life, not only on the cushion; more feeling is the sign it is working.
What does "countless little deaths" refer to?
The repeated releasing of the ego's claim on each insight. The mind appropriates the glimpse into a story; that story must be seen and let go, again and again, until the pattern runs down.
What is the mirror mind?
Meeting each moment without the mediation of past conditioning: beginner's mind, don't-know mind. Reflecting what is, without holding or preferring. To live this way is to be awake; in Christian terms, to be a new creation.
What is "freedom to"?
Beyond freedom from the character lies the freedom to evolve: to grow the vessel, expand the sphere of "I" until it includes everything, and let awareness shine through a matured human instrument. Always becoming Buddha.
