Illustration direction

Three ways to draw the same idea

Every illustration on the site has to do one job: let a reader see a mechanism they would otherwise have to assemble from prose. Below, three candidate styles are applied to the same three concepts from the path, so the choice is about the pictures rather than a description of them. The full inventory of what needs drawing follows.

Direction AInk and one gold

A single hand-drawn line, like a brush or a fine pen, with no fills except one: a gold that stands only for awareness. It sits naturally beside the Zen and Taoist sources, it stays legible at small sizes, and its restraint keeps the pictures from competing with the text. Everything that is "the show" is line; the one thing that is not the show is gold.

  • Monoline, hand-drawn feel
  • No fills except gold = awareness
  • Reads in light and dark
  • Closest to the Zen sources
  • Quietest; best beside long text

Direction BCut paper

Flat shapes with no outlines, in three tones: mist for the world, teal for energy and attention, gold for awareness. It is warmer and more immediate, it survives being shrunk to an app tile, and its shapes can be animated later (a fan slowing, a ripple spreading). It gives up some precision for friendliness.

  • Flat fills, no outlines
  • Three tones: mist, teal, gold
  • Scales down to app tiles
  • Easy to animate later
  • Friendliest; least "ancient"

Direction CBlueprint

Thin technical lines, dimension marks and small capital labels, in the spirit of the schematic deck you shared. It treats the self as a mechanism to be understood and makes the illustrations do explanatory work: arrows can be labelled, stages numbered, gaps measured. It is the most information-dense and the coolest in temperature.

  • Hairline strokes, dimension marks
  • Labelled parts and arrows
  • Matches the schematic deck
  • Most explanatory
  • Coolest; least warm for a first-time reader
